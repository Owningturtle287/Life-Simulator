# Small experiments

Keep the seed fixed, save a checkpoint, and change one condition at a time. Compare equal amounts of **model time**, not equal real-world waiting time. The graphs and field notes measure the run; there are no scripted milestones.

## 1. An inside and an outside

Start with Building blocks and the default conditions. Switch to Bonds and run at 4× or 16×. Watch amphiphile chains acquire neighbors and sometimes close. Tap an enclosure to see whether it contains information chains or just trapped chemistry. A closed vesicle alone is not classified as a model cell.

Try the same seed in a smaller chamber. Higher encounter density can change assembly, while crowding can disrupt structures. If assembly stalls, that is a result, not a loading screen.

## 2. Does geometry help?

Open Cell studio and choose “Try a guided enclosure.” The guide supplies an editable ring, a short chain, catalysts, light-capturing material, and substrates. Use Move to distort the outline, or Erase to open a gap. Watch the checklist change. Save both versions as designs, release them into comparable fresh worlds, and inspect their boundaries over time.

Neighboring compatible particles connect automatically when placed. Connect joins two matching particles within reach; it cannot join a lipid directly to RNA. A broken boundary may let enclosed material disperse. The editor does not turn a sketch into a special organism object.

## 3. Resource dependence

Use the Prokaryotic start. Save a checkpoint. Run with normal food and light, recording model time and counts. Restore, reduce illumination and nutrient influx, and run for the same model duration. Observe energy, reactions, information chains, and structural persistence. Structural cell counts can remain high even when chemistry is unfueled; use the energy view too.

## 4. Fidelity and inheritance

Begin with a fueled information chain and nearby activated nucleotides. Compare low and high copying-error settings from equivalent checkpoints. A completed strand advances a molecular generation; incomplete attempts do not. Use the lineage view and field notes. Copying is resource-limited, so mutation alone cannot cause reproduction.

## 5. Neighbors and worlds within

The Eukaryotic start provides nested analogue compartments. Zoom into them. Binding packets may connect nearby boundaries, while nested boundaries can retain different local contents. Ask whether arrangements persist under changing currents and salinity. An attached aggregate is not proof of coordinated multicellularity, and an enclosed compartment is not proof of obligate symbiosis.

## 6. Motion without an appendage part

In Cell studio, place short contractile chains near an enclosure, distribute energy sources asymmetrically, and release the design. Compare motion with and without currents. You are testing a simple active-matter approximation; no flagellum template or navigation behavior is assigned. Useful locomotion is not guaranteed.

Export a world before an intervention to preserve the exact starting state. The application retains a rolling history and recent field notes, rather than every event from an unlimited run.
